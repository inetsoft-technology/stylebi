Chart overlay surfaces — companion drawings all decided

Three decided changes, drawn

Everything here is settled — this document adds no proposals. It exists because three of the ticket's decisions are visual and were recorded as prose, and a layout described in a table gets implemented two different ways by two people. Items that are mechanical value swaps stay in the ticket where they belong; drawing them would add review weight without adding information.

01

Selection — one vocabulary, and chrome stops flooding

Two decisions in one drawing. Every selection binds to the shipped focus family, replacing the off-palette #dc581e and the annotation's dotted grey. And selecting chrome — an axis, a legend, a title — draws a stroke with no fill, while selecting data keeps the fill. The fill is area-proportional but the signal need is constant: 30% alpha is a tint on a 20px bar and a slab over a 350px axis band.

A 30% wash over the whole band, in an orange that is in no palette, covering the tick labels the user selected the axis in order to read.
Proposed — axis selected stroke only

2px --inet-primary-color, nothing inside it. The labels stay readable. Accepted trade: on a busy chart this is a quieter signal than a wash.
Data selection keeps its fill
Fill --inet-focus-ring-color, stroke --inet-primary-color. Marking values is what a fill is for, and the regions are small enough that it stays a tint. Unchanged in behaviour — only the colour moves onto the palette.

03

Resize Plot sliders

The two sliders that appear when Resize Plot is invoked — one above the plot, one rotated down its left edge. They are the only native input[type=range] in the portal, and the only chart overlay that never had a theme pass.

Today 4 problems
1A bordered box on the plot. 150×22px, 1px #ddd, no radius, 50% white fill. Reads as a form field dropped on a chart. Every other chart overlay that has been touched uses a shell surface with a radius.
2#ff8d41 is a fourth orange — brighter and pinker than the primary #E58A2A — at full chroma, over data that is supposed to be the loudest thing on the card.
3A 2px #d3d3d3 track is thinner and paler than the chart's own gridlines, so it reads as a stray gridline with a bead on it.
4The vertical copy is the horizontal one rotated −90°, so its 22px box becomes 22px of intrusion into the plot's left edge.
Proposed mostly deletion
1No container. The plot is already the surface. Deleting the box removes the border, the fill and the 22px intrusion in one edit, and the control stops competing with the card's own border.
2Track to --inet-default-border-color (#D9D5CC), pill radius. Reads as chrome rather than as a gridline that lost its way — it is deliberately darker than a gridline, not lighter.
3Thumb 12px in --inet-primary-color with a 2px white ring. The ring is the one addition: a handle that travels over bars and gridlines needs separation from whatever is behind it, and a halo costs less attention than a border.
4Length flexes. min(150px, 60% of the plot edge), floor 80px, below which the slider does not render — the plot is too small to resize meaningfully anyway.

This is smaller than what it replaces
The file is ~200 lines of range styling. Roughly half is ::-ms-* and -ms-high-contrast for IE11, which Angular 21 cannot run — delete outright. Most of the rest is the hover and focus size-change rules repeated across three engine prefixes; dropping the size change deletes six of them.
What is left is a track colour, a thumb colour, a thumb size, a hover colour and a focus ring — per engine, and the engines now agree, so the WebKit and Firefox blocks become near-identical instead of drifting (11px thumb in Firefox against 13px in WebKit today, for no stated reason).
Keep it a native input[type=range]. The portal has four div-based sliders and none of them is keyboard-reachable — vs-slider is role="slider" with tabindex="-1". Rebuilding this one to match them would trade a working control for a consistently broken one.
Scope — what is deliberately not drawn
None of this is part of the chart card spec. These surfaces are drawn over the card or inside the plot, and no §04 spacing rule reaches them; nothing in the card spec depends on any of it, and it can ship whenever.
The ticket's other items stay in prose on purpose. Annotation overlays, drill and date-comparison tips, the data-tip z-index registration, the resize handles and the remaining colour literals are value swaps with no design content — drawing them would add review weight without adding information. One caveat on ownership: the card tooltip in §02 is styled from the shell's _directives.scss and is used across the product, not only by charts. It sits in a chart ticket because that is where it was found, not because charts own it — whoever implements it should expect a review radius beyond the chart.
